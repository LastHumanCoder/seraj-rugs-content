Oushak Rugs vs Persian-Style Rugs: Old Patterns, New Colors

Oushak rugs have a look that designers keep coming back to: large open medallions, soft florals, and a palette that reads calm rather than busy. In this guide we compare oushak rugs with Persian-style rugs so you can tell the two apart and point a room in the right direction. The short version is that oushak rugs lean lighter and more spacious, while Persian-style rugs run denser and more detailed. We weave both on our own looms in Nepal and India, so oushak rugs can keep their traditional patterns while moving to the modern colors a current interior wants. If you love the bones of oushak rugs but need a fresh palette, that is exactly the kind of project we are built for.

Oushak rugs vs Persian-style rugs at a glance

The two families overlap in spirit but part ways on density and mood. Here is the simple comparison, pattern to pattern.

TraitOushak rugsPersian-style rugs
OriginWestern Anatolia, in the Oushak weaving traditionThe broad Persian design tradition, including Tabriz, Kashan and Heriz lineages
PatternLarge, open medallions and loose florals with room to breatheDense, finely drawn medallions, vines and all-over fields
PaletteSoft and faded: ivory, sand, muted terracotta, dusty blueDeeper and richer: indigo, madder red, saffron, forest tones
FeelCalm, airy, forgiving in a casual or transitional roomFormal, layered, grounding in a classic interior

Neither is better; they solve different problems. The right pick depends on how much pattern a room can carry and how formal you want it to read.

Modern oushak rugs: traditional patterns, new colors

The reason modern oushak rugs sit so well in current interiors is that the drawing is already restrained. An Oushak field leaves space between its motifs, so it does not fight a room full of texture or art. Keep that same open pattern and change the palette, and you get a rug that feels traditional in structure but contemporary on the floor.

Our partners usually start from a classic Oushak layout and then shift the ground to a warm neutral, cool the florals toward gray-blue, or drop the contrast so the medallion reads as a whisper rather than a statement. The knots, the wool and the hand are unchanged; only the color story moves. If you want to see how far a pattern can travel, our traditional collection and our modern collection show both ends of that range.

Persian style rugs and classic Persian carpet designs

Persian style rugs cover a much wider field than a single look. Classic Persian carpet designs run from tight central medallions to all-over vine scrolls and garden layouts, each tied to a weaving city with its own hand. They reward a room that can hold detail: a study, a formal living room, a space where the rug is meant to anchor rather than recede.

The Tabriz Persian rug tradition

A Tabriz Persian rug is one of the most recognized of these, known for crisp medallions, fine knotting and a balanced, symmetrical field. It is a good reference point when a project wants Persian formality but also wants the pattern reproduced cleanly at a specific size. Because we hand-knot to order, a Tabriz-inspired layout can be rewoven at your exact dimensions rather than forced to fit a stock size.

How to choose between the two

Use the room, not the rug, as your guide. A few questions settle most projects.

  • How much other pattern is in the space? Busy walls, bold art or heavy texture pair better with the open Oushak look. A quiet, minimal room can carry the density of Persian-style.
  • How formal should it read? Oushak softens and relaxes; Persian-style adds weight and ceremony.
  • What is the palette doing? If you need the rug to disappear into a calm scheme, Oushak in modern colors is the easier fit. If the rug is the main event, Persian carpet designs give you more to look at.
  • Is the room an odd size? Either pattern can be rewoven to the exact dimensions, so the shape of the room should not force the decision. See our note on a custom size rug when a room sits between standard measurements.

If a project wants something between the two, that is fine. A looser Persian layout in a faded palette, or an Oushak medallion with richer color, both live happily in the middle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Oushak rugs and Persian-style rugs?+
Oushak rugs come from the Western Anatolian Oushak tradition and favor large, open medallions, loose florals and a soft, faded palette. Persian-style rugs draw on the broader Persian design tradition and tend to be denser and more finely detailed, with richer colors. Oushak reads calm and airy; Persian-style reads formal and layered.
Are modern oushak rugs still traditional?+
Yes. Modern oushak rugs keep the traditional open pattern and hand-knotted construction and simply shift the palette to current colors, such as warm neutrals or cool gray-blues. The structure stays classic while the color story moves, which is why the look works in contemporary rooms.
What are classic Persian carpet designs?+
Classic Persian carpet designs include central medallions, all-over vine scrolls and garden layouts, each tied to a weaving region with its own hand. They carry more detail than an Oushak field and suit rooms where the rug is meant to anchor the space.
What is a Tabriz Persian rug?+
A Tabriz Persian rug comes from the Tabriz weaving tradition and is known for crisp medallions, fine knotting and a balanced, symmetrical field. It is a strong reference when a project wants clean Persian formality reproduced accurately at a specific size.
Which style suits a modern or transitional room?+
Oushak in modern colors is usually the easier fit for a modern or transitional room, because the open pattern does not compete with texture or art. Persian-style works well in a quieter, more minimal space where it can act as the main feature.
Can you weave an Oushak or Persian pattern in a custom size and color?+
Yes. We hand-knot to order on our own looms, so either pattern can be rewoven at your exact size, knot count and palette. Send a reference, a render or a palette and we will sample it, then weave to the approved strike-off.
