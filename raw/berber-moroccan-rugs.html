What berber rugs and moroccan rugs actually are

Berber rugs are named for the Amazigh (Berber) weaving communities of North Africa, where rugs have long been made by hand for warmth, seating, and bedding rather than for show. Because the tradition spans many regions, moroccan rugs are not one product. The word covers everything from a flatwoven kilim with no pile to a thick, shaggy pile rug, so a request for berber rugs can mean several very different pieces.

When our partners ask us for berber rugs, the first job is to pin down the look. Do you want the plush, cream-ground shag that most people picture, the flat geometric weave, or the colorful scrap-wool style? Each one weaves differently, wears differently, and suits a different room. Getting that clear up front is what keeps a custom rug on brief.

Moroccan shag rugs vs flat Berber weaves

The biggest split is pile height. Moroccan shag rugs, often called the Beni Ourain look, use a long, soft pile on a pale wool ground with fine dark diamond lines. A flat Berber weave is the opposite: low or no pile, with the pattern built straight into the structure. If you are weighing one against the other, our guide to low pile vs high pile rugs covers how each behaves underfoot.

A flat weave is lighter, easier to clean, and better under a dining chair. We can weave either on our own looms, so the choice is about your room, not about what is in stock.

The boho rug look, and where it comes from

The boho rug trend leans heavily on Berber design: neutral wool grounds, hand-drawn diamonds, and a slightly irregular hand that reads as made, not machined. That is why a search for a boho rug and a search for berber rugs often land on the same piece. The honest version of the look is a wool rug woven by hand, where small variations in the line are part of the character rather than a fault.

If you want the boho feel without a full cream shag, an Azilal-style pattern or a flat geometric weave gives you the motifs with a lower profile. Our modern collection shows how those geometric lines translate into current palettes.

Wool, pile, and what wears well

Most Berber and Moroccan pieces are wool, and wool is the right call for a high-traffic room: it resists crushing, hides soil, and springs back. For a shag, the pile length and the density of the knot or tuft decide how plush it feels and how well it holds up. A very long, loosely packed pile looks dramatic but mats faster; a slightly shorter, denser pile keeps the shag feel and lasts longer.

Think about the room before the pile. A bedroom or a low-use lounge can carry a deep shag carpet; an entry, a hallway, or a dining zone is better with a flat Berber weave or a dense medium pile. We weave both hand-knotted and hand-tufted constructions, and we will steer you to the one that matches the use, not the photo alone.

Sizing and placement before you order

Berber and Moroccan rugs read best when they anchor the furniture rather than float in the middle of the floor. In a living room, aim for a rug wide enough to sit under at least the front legs of the sofa and chairs. In a bedroom, a size that runs under the lower two-thirds of the bed leaves a soft border on three sides, which suits a shag especially well.

Standard sizes cover many rooms, but Berber patterns are drawn to the field, so an odd room size can crop a diamond motif awkwardly. When a room sits between two standard sizes, we weave to your exact measurements and set the pattern to the field so the motif stays balanced. Send the dimensions with the brief.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between berber rugs and moroccan rugs?+
Berber rugs are the broad family of handwoven North African rugs made by Amazigh communities. Moroccan rugs are the Moroccan part of that tradition and range from flat kilims to deep shag. In practice the terms overlap, so the useful question is which weave and pile you want, not which label.
Are moroccan shag rugs the same as Beni Ourain?+
The plush, cream-ground shag most people picture is the Beni Ourain look, so yes, moroccan shag rugs and Beni Ourain usually describe the same style: a long wool pile with fine dark diamond lines. We weave this look to order in the pile height and ground color you want.
Is a boho rug the same thing as a Berber rug?+
A boho rug often borrows Berber design, neutral wool grounds and hand-drawn diamonds, which is why the terms get used together. Not every boho rug is Berber, but a genuine handwoven Berber piece is one of the most authentic ways to get the boho look.
How do I clean a Moroccan shag carpet?+
Shake it out or vacuum on a low, no-beater setting so the long pile is not pulled. Blot spills rather than rub, and have a wool rug professionally cleaned when it needs a full wash. A denser pile mats less and is easier to keep looking even.
Do you make flat Berber weaves as well as shag?+
Yes. We weave the full range on our own looms, from a flat, low-pile Berber kilim to a deep shag of the same design. We can match the motif across both so a room can carry whichever pile height suits the use.
Can I order a Berber rug in a custom size and color?+
Yes. As a manufacturer we set pile, wool, ground color, motif scale, and size to your brief before weaving, and we can match a shade to a scheme. Send your measurements and a reference, and we sample the piece before the full order is woven.
