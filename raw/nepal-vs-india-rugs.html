Nepal or India: where to make your rugs, and why it matters

Most of the Nepal vs India rugs debate online treats the two as rivals. In our own production they are not. We run roughly 200 looms with about 500 weavers across both countries, which means the question is rarely which country is better and almost always which country suits this rug. A fine modern hand-knotted piece and a large traditional program are different builds, and the loom best set up for one is not always the one set up for the other.

Getting the origin right early saves rework later. The construction, the wool, the design detail a rug can carry, and the lead time all shift with the country. Partners who tell us the design intent and the wear the rug needs to take up front let us route each piece to the right loom from the start, rather than forcing a project onto whichever origin was decided before the design was final.

Nepal: our deep hand-knotted program and highland wool

Nepal is where our hand-knotting shows best. As a handmade rug manufacturer in Nepal, we build on a weaving tradition carried over the Himalaya and pair it with the Tibetan knot, hand-spun and hand-carded Tibetan highland wool, and the fine knot counts that detailed modern and contemporary designs need. The wool matters as much as the knot: highland wool is high in lanolin, which gives the pile luster and resilience, and keeping it hand-processed preserves the small natural variations that make a hand-knotted surface read as handmade.

What the Nepal loom is built for

  • Fine, high-knot hand-knotted rugs where design detail and density matter.
  • Modern, contemporary and abstract work, and statement pieces for designers.
  • Hand-spun Tibetan highland wool, with silk blended in where a design needs extra sheen.
  • Hospitality hand-knotted runs held to one standard across the order.

For more on this side of our work, see our Nepal handmade rugs story and our Tibetan rugs page.

India: range, scale and tradition from our own looms

India is where our range opens up. As one of the rug manufacturers in India that owns its production rather than brokering it, we make hand-knotted, hand-tufted and flatweave rugs on our own looms, which lets a single project mix constructions without moving between unrelated suppliers. Our founder, Shahid Ali Shah, brought the Tibetan knot to India, so the hand-knotted craft is not confined to Nepal; India adds breadth on top of it.

What the India loom is built for

  • Traditional and transitional patterns alongside modern work.
  • Hand-tufted rugs and flatweaves such as kilim and dhurrie, next to hand-knotted construction.
  • Larger programs and varied constructions scheduled on capacity we control.
  • Rug sourcing in India handled through one accountable maker, not a chain of traders.

You can see the construction ranges on our hand-knotted and hand-tufted collections.

Where Seraj makes each type of rug

Here is how we usually route work. Fine, detailed hand-knotted rugs, modern and contemporary designs, and anything leaning on highland wool and high knot counts tend to go to Nepal. Hand-tufted rugs, flatweaves, traditional and transitional patterns, and broader multi-construction programs tend to go to India. Many projects end up using both, which is exactly why we keep looms in each country.

How rug sourcing in India and Nepal works with us

Because we own both sides of the production, the routing is ours to manage. Partners send a brief, a render, or a design from our library, along with sizes and the wear the rug needs to handle. We confirm the construction, say which origin suits it and why, weave a strike-off for approval, and schedule the run. If a program spans both countries, we coordinate it as one order rather than two. Importing considerations such as duties and classification sit outside the design decision, and we cover those separately in our note for rug importers on Nepal's LDC graduation; current duty rates should always be verified before you rely on them.

How to choose: match the origin to the project

If you remember one thing, make it this: choose the origin after you know the rug, not before. Start from the design, the construction, and the wear, and the country tends to choose itself. A fine hand-knotted modern piece points to Nepal; a traditional hand-tufted program or a flatweave set points to India; a mixed project points to both.

When you are unsure, send us the brief and let us advise. We would rather route a piece correctly at the start than have a partner commit to an origin that fights the design. Frequently asked questions

Are Nepal rugs better than India rugs?+
Neither is better outright. Nepal is our deepest hand-knotted program, strong on fine detail, highland wool and modern work. India gives us more construction range and scale, including hand-tufted and flatweave rugs and traditional patterns. The right choice depends on the design, the construction, and the wear the rug has to take.
What is the difference between a handmade rug manufacturer in Nepal and in India?+
In Nepal our work centers on the Tibetan knot, hand-spun Tibetan highland wool and fine hand-knotted construction. In India we make hand-knotted, hand-tufted and flatweave rugs across traditional and modern designs. We own the looms in both countries, so either way you are dealing with the maker rather than a broker.
Can rug manufacturers in India also make Tibetan-knot rugs?+
Yes. Our founder, Shahid Ali Shah, brought the Tibetan knot to India, so hand-knotted craft is not limited to Nepal. India adds hand-tufted, flatweave and traditional ranges on top of hand-knotted construction, which makes it suited to broader, multi-construction programs.
How does rug sourcing in India work with Seraj?+
We own our production in India, so you work with one accountable maker instead of a chain of traders. You send a brief, render or library design with sizes and wear requirements; we confirm the construction, weave a strike-off for approval, and schedule the run on capacity we control.
Can one project use both Nepal and India?+
Yes, and many do. Where a program needs a fine hand-knotted hero piece and a set of flatweaves or hand-tufted rugs, we split the work across both origins and run it as a single order with one point of contact, so you are not managing two separate suppliers.
What about duties and importing when choosing an origin?+
Importing considerations such as duties and classification sit apart from the design decision and can change, so they should be confirmed before you rely on them. We cover the current context for Nepal in a separate note for importers, and we route each piece to the origin that suits it while keeping that information in view.
