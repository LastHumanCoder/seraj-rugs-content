What LDC graduation actually is

LDC stands for Least Developed Country, a United Nations category for economies that meet certain income, human asset and economic vulnerability thresholds. Countries move out of the category, or graduate, once they pass those thresholds for long enough. The Nepal LDC graduation is scheduled to take effect on November 24, 2026, which is why the Nepal LDC graduation is a live question for anyone who imports handmade carpets from the region.

Graduation is a milestone for the country, not a switch that rewrites the craft. The looms, the weavers and the knots are the same the day after as the day before. What can shift, over a transition period, is the set of trade preferences a graduated country qualifies for in certain markets. That is the part rug importers need to understand, and it is the part where numbers must come from current official sources rather than from a blog post.

What it may mean for rug importers

Handmade rugs cross borders under customs classifications, and the terms that apply depend on the destination country, the classification used, and whether any preference scheme is in play. Several of those terms can be linked to a country's development status. When a country graduates from LDC status, the preference programs it is eligible for may be reviewed or phased over time. The practical effect varies by market, and it is rarely instant.

Why we will not print a number

You will see figures circulating about what the Nepal carpet industry faces after graduation. We are deliberately not repeating any duty rate, tariff percentage, Nepal GSP figure or HS code rugs duty number here, because those depend on your destination market and on rules that can change. Treat any specific number you read, including ours if we printed one, as something to verify. Current duty rates and preference eligibility should be confirmed with official customs and trade authorities before you order or quote.

Classification still matters

Whatever the preference picture, the right customs classification for your product still drives the terms you pay. Confirm the correct HS code rugs classification for each construction with your broker, because a hand-knotted wool piece and a tufted piece are not always treated the same way, and the destination country has the final say.

The Nepal carpet industry is not going anywhere

It helps to separate the trade headline from the ground reality. The Nepal carpet industry is one of the oldest and most skilled hand-knotting bases in the world, and the Tibetan knot woven there is a genuine specialty. A change in a country's development status does not remove that skill or close those workshops. Demand for well-made handmade rugs is driven by designers, importers and hospitality buyers who care about construction, not by which category a country sits in.

We weave in Nepal ourselves, on our own looms, so this is not a view from the outside. The craft is intact. The conversation importers should be having is about terms and routing, not about whether the Nepal carpet industry can still deliver the work. It can.

Our two-country structure is what matters to rug importers. We are a manufacturer with our own looms in both Nepal and India, roughly 200 looms and around 500 weavers across the two countries. Two production bases give partners options that a single-country supplier cannot. That is what we mean by two-country routing: one maker, two origins, a plan that adapts to the terms you actually face rather than the ones anyone guessed at.

What to confirm before you order

Graduation is a reason to check your assumptions, not a reason to pause good sourcing. Here is a short list to work through with your own advisers before you commit.

  • Confirm current duty rates and any preference eligibility for your destination market with official customs or trade authorities. Do not rely on figures from secondary sources, including this page.
  • Confirm the correct HS code rugs classification for each construction with your customs broker.
  • Ask whether any transition or phase-in period applies after the Nepal LDC graduation for the programs you use.
  • Map which of your products could be woven in either Nepal or India, so you have a routing option ready.
  • Keep documentation of origin clean and consistent, since origin is what preference and classification rules hang on.

Will LDC graduation change the duty I pay on Nepali rugs?+
It may change which preference programs apply over time, but the effect depends on your destination market and can be phased. We do not state any duty rate here. Confirm current duty rates and preference eligibility with official customs or trade authorities before you order.
What HS code applies to handmade rugs after graduation?+
Classification depends on the construction and the destination country, and graduation does not by itself reclassify a product. Confirm the correct HS code with your customs broker for each construction rather than assuming one rate covers all rug types.
