Rug Manufacturers: 12 Questions to Ask Before You Choose One

Choosing between rug manufacturers is one of the biggest decisions a designer or trade buyer makes, and most of it is settled before a single knot is tied. The gap between rug manufacturers who own their looms and rug manufacturers who simply resell is wide, and it shows up in quality, lead time, and consistency. This guide gives you 12 questions to ask any rug manufacturer so you can tell a real maker from a trading desk. In short

  • The first question for any rug manufacturer is simple: do you own the looms, or do you broker the work to someone who does?
  • Real makers can answer about fiber, knot count, strike-offs, capacity, and lead time without checking with a third party.
  • Factory direct sourcing removes a markup and a layer of guesswork, but only when the supplier truly runs the production.
  • Ask about compliance and origin up front; these are hard to verify later and easy to confirm with an honest maker now.

Why these questions decide the whole project

Most rug problems do not start at delivery. They start at sourcing, when a buyer cannot tell a maker from a reseller. A good set of questions separates the two fast. Rug manufacturers who own their production answer in specifics: this fiber, this knot count, this loom, this lead time. Resellers answer in generalities and get back to you after a call with someone else.

We built this list from the mill floor. We run around 200 looms with roughly 500 weavers across Nepal and India, so the answers below are the ones we give our own partners. Use the 12 questions to pressure-test anyone on your list, including us.

Questions about who actually makes the rug

1. Do you own your looms, or do you place the work elsewhere?

This is the question that sorts the field. A maker owns and runs looms; a broker places your order with whoever is free. Ownership means one point of accountability for quality, timing, and changes. Ask for the count and the location of the looms, and listen for a direct answer.

2. Where are the rugs actually woven?

Origin affects knotting tradition, fiber access, and compliance. A straight answer names the country and region. We weave in Nepal and India and can tell you which construction comes from which bench, because the choice of origin is a design decision, not a label.

3. Are these genuinely handmade, and what do handmade rug manufacturers really control?

Handmade covers hand-knotted and hand-tufted, and the two behave differently in wear, price, and lead time. Genuine handmade rug manufacturers can walk you through the knot, the weaver count on a piece, and the hours behind it. If a supplier blurs the line between handmade and machine-made, treat that as an answer in itself.

Questions about materials and construction

4. What fibers and knot counts can you run?

A maker with real capability can quote a range of fibers and knot densities and tell you what each does to feel, durability, and cost of production. Ask what they recommend for high-traffic versus low-traffic use. A vague reply usually means someone else makes the decision.

5. Can you match a custom color, pattern, and size?

Standard stock is easy. Custom is where makers and resellers separate. We weave to a brief: send a render, a pattern, a palette, or an exact dimension, and we produce it. Ask whether the supplier works from your artwork or only from their own catalog.

6. How do you handle samples and strike-offs?

A strike-off is a small woven proof of your color and construction before the full run. Serious makers expect to produce one. If a supplier resists sampling or cannot explain the process, the risk moves onto you at full-run scale. We cover this in detail in our guide on rug samples and strike-offs.

Questions about capacity, lead time, and buying rug factory direct

7. What is your real production capacity and lead time?

Capacity is not a brochure number; it is how much the looms can carry while holding quality. Ask for an honest lead time for your specific size and construction, and ask what happens when volumes spike. A maker who runs the looms can answer from the schedule.

8. Can I buy rug factory direct, or only through a middleman?

Rug factory direct means you work with the people who run the production, not a trading desk between you and the loom. It removes a markup and, more importantly, a layer of guesswork on changes and timing. Ask plainly whether you are talking to the maker or to a reseller who will relay your order.

9. What happens if a batch is off-spec?

Every honest maker has a process for this. Ask who inspects, at what stage, and how a reweave or correction is handled. The answer tells you whether quality is owned or outsourced.

Questions about compliance, ethics, and shipping

10. What certifications and ethical standards do you hold?

Ask about fair-labor and material standards, and ask the supplier to name what they actually carry rather than gesture at the industry. Do not accept claims you cannot check. We cover the common marks and what they mean in our guide to Oeko-Tex and GoodWeave rugs.

11. How do you ship, and to which markets?

A maker that exports regularly can explain packing, documentation, and routing without improvising. Ask which markets they already serve and how two-country production is routed, because the origin mix affects duty and timing.

12. Will you work under an NDA and run private label?

If you are building your own line, ask whether the supplier will produce under your label and protect your designs. Makers set up for trade handle this routinely; resellers often cannot. You can read how we structure this on our trade program page.

How to compare rug manufacturers side by side

Put the same 12 questions to every supplier and score the answers, not the brochure. Specifics beat adjectives. A maker who owns the looms, samples before the run, works custom, and ships to your market will answer all 12 without a pause or a callback.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single most important question to ask rug manufacturers?+
Ask whether they own and run their own looms or place the work with a third party. Everything else, from quality control to lead time to custom work, depends on that one answer. A maker owns the production; a reseller relays your order to whoever is making it.
How can I tell real handmade rug manufacturers from resellers?+
Real handmade rug manufacturers answer in specifics: the knot type, the fiber, the knot count, the weaver count on a piece, and an honest lead time from their own schedule. Resellers answer in generalities and come back after a call with someone else. Asking for a strike-off is a quick test, since makers expect to produce one.
What does buying rug factory direct actually mean?+
Rug factory direct means you deal with the people who run the production rather than a trading desk between you and the loom. It removes a markup and a layer of guesswork on changes, samples, and timing. Confirm you are talking to the maker, not a middleman who will relay your order.
Should I choose a rug manufacturer in the USA or overseas?+
Most hand-knotted and hand-tufted production sits in weaving regions like Nepal and India rather than a rug manufacturer in the USA, because the craft and fiber access are concentrated there. What matters is whether the maker owns the looms, samples before the run, holds the standards you need, and ships reliably to your market.
Can a rug manufacturer in the UK or elsewhere handle custom work?+
Custom capability depends on whether the supplier runs its own looms, not on whether you reach a rug manufacturer in the UK, the USA, or the weaving region directly. Ask if they work from your artwork, color, and exact size, and whether they will produce a strike-off before the full run.
Why does origin matter when comparing rug manufacturers?+
Origin shapes knotting tradition, fiber access, compliance, and routing for duty and shipping. Rug manufacturers that weave across more than one country can route a project to the right bench and the right construction. Ask where each rug is actually woven rather than accepting a label.
