What we do for marine projects

Seraj Projects handles rug work for cruise, yacht and luxury-home fit-outs end to end. Four parts of that work matter most to the partners we build for.

Made to spec

Marine, cruise and yacht, cut to plan

We weave to the drawing: cruise ship carpet to the shape of a corridor or a public room, yacht rugs sized to a lounge or cabin, and bespoke pieces for a stateroom. Send a size, a render or a reference and a palette, and we return a strike-off before anything is knotted.

Durability

Finished for high-traffic marine floors

Public spaces at sea take constant foot traffic. We match wool, construction and pile height to the room, then wash and finish each piece so it wears in rather than wears out. Ask us for the fiber and finishing notes relevant to a marine fit-out.

Capacity

Two-country capacity in Nepal and India

With roughly 200 looms and 500 weavers across Nepal and India, we hold real capacity and route each order to the country and construction that fit a vessel's size and schedule. That footprint gives partners flexibility on lead time and logistics.

Handling

One project, start to finish

From the first drawing through sampling, weaving, inspection and shipping, a marine project runs through our own team, so a question about a knot count, a color shift or a schedule reaches the people who can answer it. Send us the project schedule to begin.

What we make for cruise, yacht and luxury-home fit-outs

Marine interiors ask for rugs that stock lines rarely cover: odd shapes, long runs, exact palettes and surfaces that hold up under heavy use. We make all of it to order.

Cruise ship carpet and public rooms

Corridors, lobbies and lounges on a vessel carry foot traffic every hour of the day. We weave cruise ship carpet and area pieces to the shape of the space, in constructions chosen to take that wear, and finish them so color and pile hold over time.

Yacht rugs and cabins

Yacht rugs are often cut to tight, irregular footprints. We weave to the plan, including rounds, curves and bound edges, so a lounge or cabin reads as one considered floor rather than a trimmed stock rug.

Luxury homes

The same custom capability serves luxury residences, where a designer wants an exact size, palette and pattern made once, to sign-off.

How a marine project runs

Every made-to-spec order follows the same path, so there are no surprises on the loom.

  1. You send sizes, shapes, a reference or render, a palette and the project schedule.
  2. We return a strike-off, a woven sample in your colors and quality, for approval.
  3. Once you sign it off, we weave on our own looms in Nepal or India.
  4. We inspect, wash, finish and ship, with the approved strike-off as the standard the finished pieces are held to.

Running the looms ourselves means a marine order does not pass through a middle layer, and the quality you approve is the quality that ships.

What to confirm before you order marine rugs

A short checklist worth running before you commit a marine or cruise fit-out to any maker.

Made to spec

Confirm the maker weaves to your shape and size, not stock dimensions alone. Marine floors are rarely rectangles.

Finishing for traffic

Ask how fiber, construction and finishing are matched to a high-traffic room, and request the relevant notes for a marine fit-out.

A strike-off

Insist on an approved woven sample in your colors before production. Color on a screen is not color in wool.

Capacity and schedule

Check the maker holds real loom capacity and can route the order to meet a fit-out date. Two-country capacity helps here.
