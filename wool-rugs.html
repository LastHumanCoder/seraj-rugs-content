Himalayan and New Zealand wool, and when we use each

The wool decides how a rug looks in ten years, not on day one alone. We keep two primary wools on hand and match them to the job.

Himalayan wool

Hand-carded highland wool with a high lanolin content, which gives it luster and spring underfoot. It is our default for hand-knotted pieces meant to live in a room for decades. Himalayan wool rugs wear in rather than wear out.

New Zealand wool

A cleaner, whiter staple that takes dye evenly, so new zealand wool rugs hold bright and subtle colors with less variation. We reach for it when a palette has to be exact across a large run.

For natural wool rugs, we leave the fiber undyed and let the wool's own color carry the design.

How we make a wool rug

Every custom order runs the same way, so there are no surprises on the loom.

  1. You send a size, a reference or a render, and a palette.
  2. We return a strike-off, a woven sample in your colors and quality, for sign-off.
  3. Once you approve it, we weave on our own looms in Nepal or India.
  4. We inspect, wash, finish and ship.

Because we run the looms, custom wool rugs do not pass through a middle layer, and the strike-off you approve is the standard the finished rug is held to.

What to look for in a wool rug

A short checklist worth running before you order wool rugs from anyone.

Named wool

Ask which wool and where it is from. Himalayan and New Zealand wool behave differently; a maker should tell you which you are getting.

A strike-off

Insist on an approved woven sample in your colors before production. Color on a screen is not color in wool.

Who holds the loom

Buying from the manufacturer means the quality you approve is the quality that ships, with no handoff in between.

Honest construction

Knot count, pile height and finishing should be stated plainly, not dressed up.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Himalayan and New Zealand wool rugs?+
Himalayan wool is a highland fiber with more natural lanolin, which gives it luster and spring and makes it ideal for long-lived hand-knotted rugs. New Zealand wool is a cleaner, whiter staple that takes dye very evenly, so it is our choice when a color has to be consistent across a large run. Both are durable; the right one depends on the look and the order size.
Can I order wool rugs in a custom size and color?+
Yes. Custom size, shape, pattern and palette are the core of what we do. Send a size and a reference or render, and we return a strike-off in your colors for approval before we weave.
What is a strike-off?+
A strike-off is a small woven sample made in your chosen wool, colors and quality. You approve it before full production, so the finished wool rug matches what you signed off on rather than a screen image.
Are natural, undyed wool rugs available?+
Yes. For natural wool rugs we leave the wool undyed and build the design from the fiber's own tones. It is a popular choice for partners who want a quiet, material-led look.
Do you make wool rugs in Nepal or India?+
Both. We run our own looms in Nepal and India and route each order to the country and construction that suit it, which also gives our trade partners flexibility on lead time and logistics.
Do you sell wool rugs wholesale to the trade?+
Yes. We work with interior designers, importers and hospitality buyers as a manufacturer. Start with a strike-off, or talk to us about a larger program through our trade page.
