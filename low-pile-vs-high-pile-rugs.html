In short

  • Low pile rugs are easier to vacuum, let doors and chairs clear, and hold up in busy rooms and under dining tables.

  • We weave both pile heights to order on our own looms, so pile is a spec you set, not a constraint you accept.

What rug pile height actually means

Rug pile height is the thickness of the yarn that stands up from the rug's backing, measured from the base to the top of the tuft. A flatweave such as a kilim or dhurrie has almost no pile; a dense hand-knotted rug runs low; a shag or deep hand-tufted piece runs high. Most of what people call a low pile rug lands near 6 mm, and most high pile rugs land near 15 mm, though both vary by construction and yarn.

The number sounds small, but it drives how a rug behaves day to day: how it cleans, how it sits against furniture, how it feels when you walk on it, and how long it holds its look in a busy room. If you want to see how a near-zero pile compares with a woven surface, our guide to kilim, dhurrie and flatweave rugs covers that end of the range, and you can browse constructions across the full collection.

Low pile rugs: how 6 mm behaves in a room

At around 6 mm, low pile rugs read as crisp and tailored. Patterns stay sharp because the yarn is short and tightly set, which is why dense weaves hold fine detail so well. Our hand-knotted rugs sit in this range: close to the floor, firm underfoot, and built to take years of foot traffic without matting.

Practically, a low pile clears door swings, lets dining chairs slide in and out, and gives castors and wheels a stable surface. It vacuums quickly, dries faster after a spill, and tends to show fewer footprints than a deep pile. The trade-off is warmth: a 6 mm surface is less cushioned, so a room that is meant to feel soft and enveloping may want more height.

High pile rugs and plush pile carpet: how 15 mm changes a room

At around 15 mm, high pile rugs change the feel of a floor the moment you step on it. The extra length holds air, so the surface is warmer, softer and quieter, and a deep, plush pile carpet reads as cozy and informal in a way a tight weave never will. Many of our hand-tufted rugs can be built with a taller pile for exactly this reason.

Height has costs. A high pile traps more grit between the fibers, so it needs deeper, slower cleaning. New wool pile sheds for a while before it settles. And in a heavy lane, such as a hallway or the path from a sofa to a door, a tall pile flattens and shows tracks sooner than a low one. High pile belongs where comfort matters more than wear: bedrooms, lounges and reading corners rather than entryways and under dining tables.

Low pile vs high pile rugs: a side-by-side comparison

The same room can want opposite answers depending on how it is used. This table sets the two pile heights against the factors that usually decide the call.

FactorLow pile rugs (around 6 mm)High pile rugs (around 15 mm)
Pile heightShort, close to the floorTall, deep surface
Feel underfootFirm, crisp, tailoredSoft, warm, cushioned
Best roomDining rooms, entryways, kitchens, offices, hallwaysBedrooms, lounges, reading corners, nurseries

TrafficHolds up well in busy, high-traffic lanesBest in low to moderate traffic; flattens in busy paths

Neither pile is better in the abstract. The right height is the one that fits how the room is actually used, which is where a maker helps: we can hold a pattern and palette steady and change only the pile to suit each space.

Choosing pile height by room and traffic

Start with how hard the floor works. A dining room, an entry or a kitchen sees chairs, spills and constant feet, so a low pile is the safer call. A bedroom or a lounge is about comfort, so a higher pile earns its place. For open-plan rooms, many of our partners run a low pile in the walking lanes and a higher pile in the seating zone.

Pile height also pairs with size and layering. Getting the footprint right matters as much as the height, so it is worth reading our standard rug sizes guide alongside this one. And if you like the softness of a high pile but need durability underneath, layering a knotted piece over a flatweave gives you both in one floor.

Frequently asked questions

What is rug pile height?+
Rug pile height is the thickness of the yarn that stands up from the rug's backing, measured from the base to the top of the tuft. Low pile is around 6 mm, high pile is around 15 mm or more, and a flatweave has almost no pile at all.
Are low pile rugs better for high traffic?+
Usually, yes. Low pile rugs are short and tightly set, so they resist matting, vacuum quickly and show fewer footprints. That makes them a strong choice for dining rooms, entryways, kitchens and hallways where feet, chairs and spills are constant.
What counts as high pile rugs?+
High pile rugs have a tall, deep surface, often around 15 mm or more, including shag and deep hand-tufted styles. They feel soft and warm underfoot and suit bedrooms and lounges, but they need deeper cleaning and flatten faster in busy paths.
Is a plush pile carpet hard to clean?+
A plush pile carpet is not hard to clean, but it takes more effort than a low pile. The longer fibers hold more grit, so it wants slower, deeper vacuuming and the occasional deep clean. New wool pile also sheds for a while before it settles.
Which pile height is best for a dining room?+
A low pile is best under a dining table. Chairs slide in and out without catching, crumbs and spills clean up quickly, and the firm surface stays stable under chair legs. A high pile tends to catch chairs and trap food in a dining setting.
Can you make a rug in a custom pile height?+
