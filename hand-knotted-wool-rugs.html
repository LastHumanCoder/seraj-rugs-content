What hand knotting really means

A hand-knotted rug is built one knot at a time. A weaver ties each tuft of wool around the warp threads by hand, then packs the rows down and shears the pile. Nothing is glued or punched in, which is why hand knotted wool rugs can stay in a room for decades and be repaired rather than replaced.

Knot count, stated plainly

Knot count is the honest measure of a hand-knotted rug. More knots mean a finer, tighter surface that holds crisp detail; fewer knots give a softer, more relaxed look. We weave up to 100 knots and tell our partners the real count for every piece, so a drawing with fine detail is matched to a density that can actually carry it.

How we weave a hand-knotted order

Every custom order runs the same way, so there are no surprises on the loom.

  1. You send a size, a reference or a render, a palette and a target knot count.
  2. We return a strike-off, a woven sample in your colors and quality, for sign-off.
  3. Once you approve it, we knot the full order by hand on our own looms.
  4. We inspect, wash, finish and ship, and hold the finished rug to the sample you approved.

Because we own the looms, your hand knotted wool rugs do not pass through a middle layer, and the strike-off you approve is the standard the finished rug is measured against.

What to look for in hand knotted wool rugs

A short checklist worth running before you order hand-knotted rugs from anyone.

A stated knot count

Ask for the real number. A hand knotted rug manufacturer should name the knots per square unit, not describe the pile in adjectives.

A strike-off

Insist on an approved woven sample in your colors and quality before production. Color and detail on a screen are not color and detail in wool.

Named wool

Ask which wool and where it is from. Highland wool carries detail and springs back, which is what makes a fine knot count worth paying for.

Who holds the loom

Buying from the manufacturer means the quality you approve is the quality that ships, with no handoff in between.

Frequently asked questions

What makes hand knotted wool rugs different from tufted or machine-made rugs?+
Hand-knotted rugs are tied one knot at a time around the warp, with no glue or backing holding the pile in. That construction is more durable and repairable than tufted or machine-made rugs, and it is the reason a good hand-knotted piece can last for decades.
What knot counts do you weave?+
We weave hand-knotted rugs at densities up to 100 knots. Higher counts give a finer surface that holds crisp detail; lower counts give a softer, more relaxed look. We tell you the real count for every piece so the design is matched to a density that can carry it.
Can you handle large or repeat orders of hand knotted area rugs?+
Yes. In one recent run we completed 1,200 sq m of 100-knot rugs in three months on our own looms. With roughly 200 looms and 500 weavers across Nepal and India, we hold the capacity for collections and repeat programs.
Do you make hand knotted silk rugs as well as wool?+
Our core is hand-knotted wool, often with a silk or art-silk highlight to lift detail where a design calls for it. Tell us the look you want in your brief and we will advise on the fiber mix and the knot count that suit it.
What is a strike-off and why does it matter?+
A strike-off is a small woven sample made in your chosen wool, colors, knot count and quality. You approve it before full production, so the finished rug matches what you signed off on rather than a screen image. It is how we get color and detail right before weaving the full order.
Do you sell hand knotted wool rugs wholesale to the trade?+
Yes. We work with interior designers, importers and hospitality buyers as a manufacturer. Start with a strike-off, or talk to us about a larger program through our trade page.
