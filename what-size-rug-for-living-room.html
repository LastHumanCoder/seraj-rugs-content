In short

  • For most living rooms the answer is an 8x10 or 9x12 ft rug; larger open-plan rooms often want 10x14 or bigger.
  • Decide the placement first: all furniture legs on the rug, front legs only, or the rug floating in front of the seating.
  • Measure the seating group, then add a margin so the rug reads as one grounded zone rather than a small island.
  • If your room sits between two standard sizes, a custom size fits the space instead of forcing the space to fit the rug.

How to choose your living room carpet size

Before you pick a number, pick a rule. Your living room carpet size follows from one decision: how much of the seating sits on the rug. Lay out your sofa and chairs the way you want them, measure the full seating group from the outer edges, then add a margin on each side so the rug frames the group rather than hiding under it. As a starting point, leave 8 to 18 inches of bare floor between the rug and the walls so the room still breathes.

There is no single correct living room carpet size, only the one that matches your layout. The nine layouts below cover the setups we see most often, each with measurements in feet and centimeters so you can take them straight to a tape measure.

Front legs on, all legs on, or floating

The three placements in the table are worth understanding on their own, because they decide the size more than the room does.

Front legs on the rug

The sofa and chairs each rest their front legs on the rug, with the back legs on the floor. This is the most forgiving setup and the one most living rooms use. It links the furniture into one group without needing the largest rug in the range.

All legs on the rug

Every piece sits fully on the rug, leaving a border of floor around the whole group. This reads as the most generous and settled look, and it usually pushes you up a size, from an 8x10 to a 9x12, or from a 9x12 to a 10x14.

Floating in front of the seating

Only the coffee table and foot traffic land on the rug. Keep the rug at least as wide as the sofa so it does not look stranded; a 5x8 earns its place in a small room.

Rug runner sizes and carpet sizes for halls and edges

A living room rarely sits alone. The hallway that feeds it and the open-plan kitchen beside it both read better when their rugs are sized with the same care. Common rug runner sizes run from about 2.5 x 8 ft (75 x 240 cm) up to 2.5 x 12 ft (75 x 365 cm) and longer, and they are the quiet workhorses that carry foot traffic without crowding a space.

Hallway runner size

For a hallway runner size, aim to leave 3 to 5 inches of floor on each long side and about 18 inches of bare floor at each end. In a standard hallway that lands you on a 2.5 ft wide runner, with the length cut to the corridor; long halls are the clearest case for a made-to-measure runner rather than a stock length.

Frequently asked questions

What size rug for living room is the most common?+
An 8x10 or 9x12 ft rug suits most living rooms. The 8x10 works well when you want the front legs of the sofa and chairs on the rug, while the 9x12 lets every leg sit on it for a more grounded look. Larger open-plan rooms often move up to a 10x14.
What living room carpet size fits a sectional?+
For most L-shaped sectionals a 9x12 ft rug lets both runs of the sectional rest their front legs on the rug, which ties the piece to the floor. A large U-shaped or facing-sofa layout usually wants a 10x14 so the central table and foot traffic stay on the rug.
Should all the furniture legs sit on the rug?+
Not necessarily. Front legs on the rug is the most common and forgiving setup and needs a smaller size. All legs on looks the most generous but usually pushes you up one size. A floating rug with no legs on suits compact rooms, as long as the rug is at least as wide as the sofa.
What are standard rug runner sizes?+
Common rug runner sizes run from about 2.5 x 8 ft (75 x 240 cm) up to 2.5 x 12 ft (75 x 365 cm) and longer. The width stays near 2.5 ft; the length is what you match to the hallway or galley space the runner has to cover.
What hallway runner size do I need?+
Leave 3 to 5 inches of floor on each long side of the runner and about 18 inches bare at each end. In a standard hallway that means a runner around 2.5 ft wide, with the length cut to the corridor. Long or irregular halls are the clearest case for a made-to-measure runner.
Can I order a living room rug in a size that is not standard?+
Yes. We weave to any dimension, so if your room sits between two standard carpet sizes you can order the exact size you need. A custom size is no harder for us to make than a standard one; send the measurements and we will quote and sample it.
