What shaped rugs actually are

Most shaped rugs on the market are rectangles in disguise. A mill weaves a standard panel, then a finisher cuts a circle or a curve out of it and binds the raw edge. That works for a simple round, but the pattern was drawn for a rectangle, so it rarely lands right once the corners are gone, and a cut edge is always a weaker edge.

We work the other way around. Because we run our own looms in Nepal and India, with roughly 200 looms and 500 weavers across the two, we draw the design to the final outline and weave to that shape from the first row. The border follows the curve, the field is centered inside the real silhouette, and the edge is woven and selvaged rather than cut. That is the difference between irregular shaped rugs that look intentional and ones that look trimmed.

Nine custom shaped rugs we can weave to order

These are the nine shapes we weave most often. Any of them can be hand-knotted or hand-tufted, in the size and palette you specify.

1. Round

A true circle, woven to a single diameter. Good under a round dining table or centered in an entry, where a rectangle would point at the corners of the room.

2. Oval

A softer footprint for long rooms and passages. The oval carries a border well and reads calmer than a rectangle in a passage or at the foot of a bed.

3. Octagon

Eight straight sides. It holds a geometric field cleanly and suits a formal room that wants structure without hard corners.

4. Scalloped edge

A repeating curved border, woven as part of the rug rather than stitched on. More on scalloped rugs below.

5. Half-moon

A semicircle for a hearth, a doorway, or the front of a kitchen sink. The straight side sits flush to the wall or counter and the curve opens into the room.

6. Organic freeform edge

An outline with no fixed geometry, drawn to follow the shape of the space or a design you send. This is the heart of organic shaped rugs, covered in its own section.

7. Hide silhouette

The outline of a hide or pelt, woven in wool instead of using a real skin. It gives the relaxed shape some rooms want without the animal product.

8. L-shape

A single rug that wraps a corner, an island, or a built-in seating nook, so two zones read as one floor rather than two separate mats.

9. Cut-corner and notched

A rectangle or oval with corners cut back or a notch woven in to clear a fireplace, a column, or a cabinet. The rug fits the room's obstacles instead of being pushed away from them.

Scalloped rugs and curved edges

Scalloped rugs are the shape we are asked about most, and they are the clearest case for weaving rather than cutting. A scallop is a run of repeating curves around the border. If you cut that profile out of a finished panel, every curve ends in a bound raw edge that wears and fuzzes. When we weave the scallop, the curve is selvaged like any other woven edge, so it holds up to real traffic.

We can scale the scallop to the rug: large, slow curves for a calm look, or tighter scallops for more detail. The same approach covers wavy borders, petal edges, and other curved outlines. For curved edges with a lot of detail we usually suggest hand-tufted construction, which gives the carving and the border precise control.

How we weave an organic shaped rug

Organic shaped rugs have no symmetry to lean on, so the outline is the whole job. We start from your drawing, a measured sketch, or a render, and turn it into a weaving map at full scale. The weaver follows that map row by row, building the edge as the rug grows rather than finding it later with a knife.

Because the shape is drawn before a single knot is tied, the pattern inside it is placed to suit the real outline: a color pool sits where you want it, a border thins or thickens to follow the curve, and the balance is set for the shape you are actually getting. If you are after a loose, painterly field to go with a freeform edge, our abstract rugs guide shows how we handle that kind of design.

Placing and living with an irregular shaped rug

A shape changes how a rug sits in a room, so a few placement notes help before you order.

  • Anchor the shape to a fixed feature. A round centers on a table or a light; a half-moon sits to a wall or a hearth; an L-shape follows the furniture it wraps.
  • Leave an even margin of floor around curves so the outline reads clearly. A curved rug crowded to the walls loses its shape.
  • Use a shaped pad cut to match. A rectangular pad poking out under a round or organic edge is the most common thing that spoils the look.
  • For long, narrow runs, a shaped or curved runner can work better than a circle; see our custom runner rugs for that case.

From sketch to loom: ordering custom shaped rugs

Ordering custom shaped rugs with us is the same process as any other custom piece, with one extra step for the outline. Send the shape as a measured drawing, a render, or even a photo of the space with dimensions marked. We confirm the outline, the construction, the pile, and the palette, then weave a strike-off so you can sign off on color and quality before the full rug runs.

There is no shape penalty on a loom the way there is in a finishing shop: weaving a round or a scallop takes the same craft as weaving a rectangle, and the result is a cleaner, longer-lasting edge. Start from our custom rugs page, or browse the full shop to see our constructions first.

Frequently asked questions

What are irregular shaped rugs?+
Irregular shaped rugs are rugs with an outline other than a plain rectangle: rounds, ovals, octagons, scallops, half-moons, organic edges, hide silhouettes, L-shapes and cut-corner shapes. We weave the rug to its outline from the first row rather than cutting the shape out of a finished rectangle.
Do you cut shaped rugs from a rectangle or weave them to shape?+
We weave them to shape. The design is drawn to the final outline and woven on our own looms, so the border follows the curve and the edge is selvaged rather than cut and bound. A woven edge holds up to traffic far better than a cut one.
Are scalloped rugs woven as a real scallop?+
Yes. We weave the scallop as part of the rug, so each curve is a finished woven edge. We can scale the scallops larger or tighter to suit the room, and we usually suggest hand-tufted construction for curved borders with fine detail.
Can you weave an organic shaped rug from my own drawing?+
Yes. Send a measured sketch, a render, or a photo of the space with dimensions, and we turn it into a full-scale weaving map. The weaver builds the edge as the rug grows, and the pattern inside is placed to suit the real outline.
What sizes can a shaped rug be made in?+
Any size. Because every shaped rug is custom, you give us the dimensions you need and we weave to them. There is no standard-size limit the way there is with stock rugs.
Which construction is best for a custom shaped rug?+
Rounds and ovals work well in both hand-knotted and hand-tufted. For scalloped, organic, or heavily carved edges we usually recommend hand-tufted, which gives precise control over curves. We will advise on the right construction once we see your shape and design.
